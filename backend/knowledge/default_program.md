# Default weekly training program

This is the default shape of a vertical jump training week. Use it whenever someone asks for a program, a weekly plan, or how to structure their training, unless they state a condition that matches one of the variants further down.

## The default week

Monday: high-intensity lower body
1. Warmup, going from general to specific: knee extension isometrics (3 to 5 sets of 30 to 45 seconds at about 70% effort), then dynamic flexibility (leg swings, bodyweight squats), then an empty-barbell warmup of the day's lifts.
2. Sprints and/or plyos: optional but recommended.
3. Explosive lift, usually an Olympic lift, most often the power clean. Alternatives: snatch, hang clean, or loaded jumps such as hex bar jumps or barbell squat jumps.
4. Squat pattern, most commonly a barbell back squat or front squat. Alternatives: half back squat, belt squat, or a single-leg lift such as a high box step-up or barbell lunge.
5. Posterior chain: some subset of RDL, hip thrust, hamstring curl, Nordic curl.
6. Calves: standing, seated, or an isometric calf raise variation.

Tuesday: rest, or an upper body / general day (e.g. shoulder care circuit, a push, a pull, a shoulder exercise).

Wednesday: a second high-intensity lower body day with the same slots as Monday, but with different exercise choices to fill gaps left by Monday and add variation (e.g. front squat if Monday was back squat, Nordics and calf isometrics if Monday was RDLs and seated calf raises).

Thursday: rest, or upper body / general.

Friday and Saturday: depending on the cycle, these are interchangeable between lifting and jumping, but always include a jump session if the athlete is healthy. Some cycles have no third lift that week: rest two days, then jump on Saturday. When there is a third lift, it can be a light "feel good" lift (slow, heavy-ish squats, then light posterior chain and calf work) rather than another high-intensity day.

Jump session: warm up, then progress from low-effort to 100% jumps, then do full-effort approach jumps. Stop as soon as jump quality clearly drops.

Sunday: off.

## Variants: only use when the condition applies

Only depart from the default week when the person states (or clearly implies) one of these conditions, and say which condition you're adapting for.

- Complete beginner with no training history: lift Monday, Wednesday, Friday with the same basic lifts each day (back squat, RDL, band walks, calf raise), no sprints or plyos yet, plus easy jump sessions. Source: [If You're New To Vertical Jump: START HERE, 3:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hhl7vONBAEs&t=181s) and [10:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hhl7vONBAEs&t=637s).
- In season, or only 2 days a week available: one jump day (30 to 45 minutes) and one lift day focused on fundamentals (squat, hinge, calf raise, Olympic lift if able). Source: [A Practical 2 Day Per Week Jump System, 0:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1sQ-FOxDP5M&t=58s).
- Unloading before a peak, or after months of hard training: heavy power cleans and squats on Monday, a very easy Wednesday (slow squats, band walks, slow calf raises), a high-quality jump session on Friday. Source: [How to Add Inches to Your Vertical So Fast It Feels Like Cheating, 5:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6P6ONA5ELyA&t=302s).
- Blocks with heavy plyometric or eccentric loading: jump only twice a week ("load management"). Source: [THP Vertical Jump Training Day 2: Legs, 7:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l6_VpbBSgzY&t=460s).
- Injured or managing pain: keep the structure, but swap the aggravating lifts and lower jump volume; follow the injury-specific videos.
- A specific named method or protocol (e.g. Soviet or shock-method plans, a particular athlete's custom program, programs for other sports): only describe these when the person asks about them by name or context. Never use them as the default answer to a general program question.
